From 300 Pages to Half a Page

The Sound Transit on-time performance case study.

AAIM Focus
AAIMK

Engagement Overview

Sound Transit was building a brand-new light rail line through downtown Seattle — over a dozen stations end to end — and, before a single train ran in revenue service, issued an RFP for roughly 50 reports to monitor the operation. It was a reporting philosophy built for a pre-IoT world, applied to a system that was about to generate sensor-driven operational data every few seconds. Once trains started running, on-time performance came in under 55% across all runs, throughout the day, and the agency's own reporting couldn't tell anyone why. Findings arrived as 300-page transaction log reports with zero aggregation — sometimes literally printed on paper.

The Challenge

With no established practice for interrogating the numbers, sub-55% on-time performance got treated as one undifferentiated problem. In reality, the systems responsible for train monitoring, safety, and speed control along the alignment were all working exactly as designed — the problem was invisible precisely because nobody could see past the volume of unaggregated data to find where in the system it was actually occurring.

The Solution: Aggregation That Fits on Half a Page

Adventag organized Sound Transit's ARINC-based train-tracking data into a warehouse that could actually be queried, not just archived — replacing page after page of transaction logs with a live, half-page operational view. The moment the data could be sliced by direction of travel, the story changed completely: northbound trains were late roughly 70% of the time, while southbound trains ran on time about 95% of the time. Blended together across a full day of service, those two very different realities averaged out to the sub-55% number that had been masking the real problem.

The Solution: Start Time, Not Run Time

Isolating the problem by direction was only half the answer. Once the data was cut further, it showed the trains weren't running long once they were underway — they were starting late. The southernmost station on the line doubled as the operators' break stop, and the delay was happening entirely before a train ever left it: operators weren't tracking how long they'd spent on a break before starting their run north. It had nothing to do with the monitoring, safety, or speed-control systems along the alignment, all of which were functioning exactly as designed.

Mapping to the AAIM Method

Insight

Aggregating ARINC train-tracking data into a warehouse turned an unreadable stack of transaction logs into a single view that could be split by direction and by segment. That's what let a problem that looked uniform — sub-55% on time, full stop — reveal itself as two entirely different problems hiding behind one blended number.

Knowledge

Once the root cause was pinned to start time at the southern terminus rather than anything mechanical along the route, the fix was almost anticlimactic: a simple monitoring presence at that one station. The problem disappeared quickly, and Sound Transit walked away with something more durable than a fixed number — the knowledge of exactly where, in the system, a schedule could quietly fail.

Outcome

Sometimes the metric you're trying to improve doesn't need new systems, new sensors, or new software — it needs data organized well enough to be seen, not just logged. Once Sound Transit could see the actual pattern instead of a fog of unaggregated reports, the fix took days, not another RFP cycle.
