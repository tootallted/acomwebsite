The AAIM Method

Acquire · Automate · Insight · Mediate · Knowledge

AAIMK

Data engagements, by nature, lead people down paths they wish they knew better than to take in the first place. AAIM is a methodology tested and proven at companies big and small to correct that trend — planning and delivering data-focused initiatives that turn your data and applications into a catalyst for real action and change.

Acquire

So many failed projects come down to never getting your hands on the crux of what you actually need to make decisions. If you can't get it, you can't depend on it — assuming data will be there is dangerous, because you end up building time, money, and reliance on top of an assumption.

Acquisition today is open, secured, or political — each with its own benefits and drawbacks — and the Acquire step must be an intentional, tangible process, not an afterthought.

Automate

Acquisition is expensive. Once you've acquired data, you need to be able to do it again and again without losing time or spending money twice. We intentionally invest in automating acquisition so we do it once, do it right, and never repeat the work.

Spending a bit more up front pays off over the long term — automation only has staying power when it's built intentionally.

Insight

With automation in place, an army of information is at your fingertips — no pausing to reacquire. That lets you move quickly to analyze, decide, and figure out what changes your operations and strategy actually need.

This is often the most contested part of the process: knowing which projects matter, and being able to say why, with real attributes you can rank against each other.

Mediate

No decision or data-driven statement is ever met with 100% buy-in. Data isn't just technical — it's emotional, and how people feel about the data they produced often matters more than the data itself.

Expect your data to miss the mark sometimes. It will never be perfect. The ability to mediate the next step forward is what keeps an initiative moving.

Knowledge

Following this process repeatedly builds knowledge — and knowledge is the point. We don't acquire data and processes for their own sake; it's all in service of acquiring knowledge you can act on next time.

Being able to point to what you'd change, and why, is proof you've actually gained knowledge from the work — not just repeated it.
